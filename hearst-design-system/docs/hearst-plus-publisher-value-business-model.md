# Hearst+: a publisher-first business model

**Decision brief · 25 October 2026**

For the paid-tier question, see the [Hearst+ subscription feasibility study](./hearst-plus-subscription-feasibility-study.md).

## The decision

Hearst+ can make discovery across Hearst’s magazine brands more useful and create a new daily habit. It can also move reading, advertising, subscriptions, and reader relationships away from the original brand sites. The business model must make source-brand value an explicit outcome, rather than assuming that a logo, source link, or canonical URL is enough.

**Recommendation:** Position Hearst+ as a portfolio discovery and personalization layer. Default to short, source-forward previews that send readers to the original brand site for the full experience. Allow in-app reading, watching, or browsing only where the originating brand has approved the format, the search treatment, the monetization, the audience-data rules, and the value credit. Pilot this model with source-level holdouts and expand only when each participating brand meets its agreed benefit guardrails.

No design can promise that every brand will always gain. The practical commitment should be: **no broad rollout without evidence of net value for each participating publisher, no hidden transfer of value to the aggregator, and a pause or change when a brand’s guardrail is missed.**

## What “benefit the original site” means

These outcomes are related, but they are not interchangeable:

1. **Source identity:** The reader can see which brand and editorial team created the content.
2. **Referral:** The reader clicks from Hearst+ to the original brand site.
3. **Engaged visit:** The source page loads and the reader meaningfully consumes it.
4. **Publisher economics:** The source brand gains advertising value, subscription or registration conversions, commerce value, or a defined share of Hearst+ revenue for licensed in-app consumption.
5. **Incremental audience value:** The brand gains readers or future direct visits it would not otherwise have had.

A canonical URL addresses a search-engine URL preference for similar pages. It does not redirect a reader, create a visit, guarantee a search result, record a page view, or transfer revenue. Google says canonical annotations are signals that it may interpret, and specifically says `rel=canonical` is not recommended to resolve duplication from syndication partners because syndicated pages are often different; it points to blocking indexing as the more effective approach. Set the search policy for each content mode with the brand’s SEO owners; do not rely on a global cross-domain canonical rule. ([Google: canonicalization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization), [Google: syndicated-content canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting))

## What the evidence tells us—and what it does not

The research does not support a blanket claim that aggregation either helps or harms every publisher. Results depend on the market, audience, amount of content shown, publisher size, and whether the aggregator links out or fulfills the whole content need itself.

| Evidence | Finding | Limit for Hearst+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roos, Mela & Shachar, *Journal of Marketing Research* (2020), five U.S. celebrity-news sites and browsing data from Q4 2009 | Their model estimates that inter-site links increased the chance of visiting a linked site and produced a small positive aggregate traffic effect across the five sites. Some individuals became less likely to visit a linked site. The authors say the result is limited to the particular category, period, and experienced-reader sample. | Supports testing links and previews as discovery. It does not establish that an in-app full-article reader increases the source brand’s traffic. [Study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022243720913029) |
| Athey, Mobius & Pal, NBER Working Paper 28746, Spain’s Google News shutdown | The shutdown was followed by about 20% lower overall news consumption among affected users and about 10% fewer page views on publishers other than Google News; the decline was concentrated among smaller publishers, while large publishers showed no significant overall traffic change. | Suggests an aggregator can expand consumption while effects vary by publisher. It studies an external news aggregator, not a single owner’s multi-brand magazine portfolio. [Study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28746) |
| Calzada & Gil, *Marketing Science* (2019), Spain and Germany | Their analysis reports that the shutdown reduced online ad revenue and advertising intensity at news outlets; an opt-out in Germany reduced visits to Axel Springer-controlled outlets by 8%. Their broader finding is a net market-expansion effect on outlet visits. | Another external-news context; it does not measure full-content in-app consumption or Hearst’s internal revenue allocation. [Study](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2019.1150) |
| Jeon & Nasr, *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics* (2016) | Their model identifies readership expansion versus business stealing; publisher profits are ambiguous when the two effects compete. | Explains why aggregate readership or traffic is not enough to infer every brand’s profit. [Study](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/mic.20140151) |

**Evidence boundary:** These studies examine news ecosystems and independent sites, not 29 magazine brands from one company. They offer hypotheses and warning signs; they are not a Hearst forecast. No reviewed source establishes that every Hearst brand would gain from Hearst+.

## Where the aggregator can help or hurt

### Ways it can help

- **New-to-brand discovery:** A reader arrives for one interest and encounters a title they had not considered.
- **Better matching:** A short preview helps a reader decide that a specific story is worth opening.
- **Cross-brand portfolio reach:** Different titles become discoverable in one place without requiring readers to know each publication in advance.
- **Incremental monetization:** Hearst+ can create new advertising, subscription, membership, commerce, or partnership revenue that can be shared with the source brand.
- **Stronger return behavior:** A useful daily destination may bring readers back to Hearst-owned content who otherwise would not visit any Hearst site that day.

### Ways it can hurt

- **Substitution:** A complete story or gallery in Hearst+ can replace the source-site visit and its ad impressions.
- **Lost direct relationship:** The app can bypass a brand’s newsletter, registration, app-install, subscription, or membership path.
- **Value shifts between titles:** A portfolio metric can rise while a particular title, format, or reader segment loses traffic or revenue.
- **Search competition:** Duplicate or near-duplicate app pages can compete with the source if the indexing policy is wrong. A canonical tag alone is not reliable syndication protection.
- **Brand dilution:** A generic presentation can leave readers remembering Hearst+ but not the source brand or author.
- **Rights mismatch:** Article text, photography, video, gallery assets, community posts, and commerce content may each have different rights and monetization terms. A source URL does not grant permission to republish.
- **Data concentration:** If Hearst+ keeps all behavioral data while brands receive only a logo credit or click count, the portfolio gains an audience asset while the originating title loses a direct relationship.

The risk is not uniform across 29 brands. Their content formats, audience overlap, subscription models, ad economics, freshness needs, and role in the portfolio differ. Each brand therefore needs an explicit participation mode and outcome scorecard.

## Recommended product and commercial model

### 1. Use a rights-aware content participation model

Every content item should resolve to an originating brand and a permitted use mode. The mode is a commercial and editorial decision, not something inferred from the existence of a URL.

| Mode | Hearst+ experience | Publisher value path | Default search posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Discovery preview** | Headline, brand, byline/date, image allowed for the preview, and a concise excerpt or original summary | Direct click to the original site; source campaign can be measured | Do not publish a full duplicate; brand SEO defines treatment |
| **Licensed in-app article** | Full text and approved media in a Hearst+ reader | Agreed share, per-engaged-consumption fee, or minimum guarantee; visible source identity and a source-site path remain | Brand SEO approves index/noindex and canonical behavior for the actual page |
| **Licensed video/gallery** | Player or gallery appears in Hearst+ under explicit format-specific rights | Source-side player/ad measurement where supported, or a defined in-app revenue credit | Handle the page and media’s search policy by format |
| **Owned Hearst+ original** | Hearst+ commissions or materially creates a distinct item | Hearst+ economics, with clear editorial ownership; source brands can still be recommended where relevant | Self-canonical only if it is genuinely the primary, distinct page |
| **Community contribution** | Reader or host conversation with clearly named community and brand | Community participation and retention; do not label seeded or hosted material as reader-generated | Apply community and user-generated-content search rules separately |

For the first rollout, **discovery preview should be the default**. Full in-app consumption should require an affirmative brand agreement. Premium, exclusive, paywalled, high-converting, or strategically important content can remain source-only unless the brand chooses otherwise. The Association of Online Publishers’ syndication guidance similarly recommends selecting content deliberately, defining usage rights, sharing performance data, and retaining flexibility to adjust. ([AOP: Good Practice Principles for Content Syndication](https://www.ukaop.org/assets/downloads/AOP-Good-Practice-Principles-for-Content-Synidication.docx.pdf))

### 2. Make referrals real and attributable

- Keep the source brand visible on every card and detail page; retain byline, date, image credit, and original source URL where available.
- Use an obvious action such as **“Continue on [Brand]”** that goes straight to the original story—no link shortener, redirect wall, or forced intermediate page.
- Add stable campaign parameters agreed by the brand analytics teams. Do not put user IDs, email, interests, or other personal data in outbound URLs.
- Record the outbound click on Hearst+ and, where possible, reconcile it with a landing event in the source-site analytics or server logs.
- Report an outbound click as a click. Report an engaged page visit only when the destination observes one. GA4 documents outbound-click events and campaign parameters as separate measurement steps; neither by itself proves a business outcome. ([GA4: outbound clicks](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13566436), [GA4: campaign URLs](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952))
- If the reader stays in Hearst+, credit the source brand through the commercial model and an agreed reporting method. Do not describe that as a source-site page view.

### 3. Share the value created by in-app consumption

An in-app read can be valuable even without an original-site visit, but that value needs to be recognized and allocated. Agree on a per-brand method before launch. Options include:

- A share of net Hearst+ advertising or sponsorship revenue attached to the content.
- A fee for a qualifying, engaged in-app consumption, with a minimum guarantee for licensed inventory.
- A negotiated content license plus incremental referral and conversion reporting.
- A hybrid that assigns an agreed value to both source-site visits and on-platform engagement.

There is no evidence-based universal rate in the reviewed research. Finance, advertising, subscriptions, and brand leadership should define the rate and guard against counting the same value twice. The source brand should receive a monthly statement with the inputs, exclusions, and adjustments behind its credit.

### 4. Make publisher value part of feed ranking

The river should optimize for reader usefulness and publisher value together, subject to editorial quality, freshness, and variety. Ranking purely for time in Hearst+ risks favoring content that keeps readers in the app at the source brand’s expense. A source-forward experience should give discovery opportunities to brands that match the reader, while keeping per-title exposure, referrals, and in-app consumption visible and adjustable.

Editorial and commercial teams should be able to set brand-level participation, rights, excluded formats, commercial credit rules, freshness limits, and pause status. These controls are portfolio governance requirements, not a request to change the current prototype.

## Measurement plan and rollout gates

### Baseline before the pilot

For each pilot brand and format, agree on a baseline period and capture:

- Source-site sessions, engaged sessions, page views, and engaged time.
- Ad impressions, viewability where available, revenue, and yield.
- Subscription starts, registrations, newsletter signups, and app installs.
- New versus returning audiences and direct traffic where the brand can report it.
- Existing source traffic from the other Hearst properties, to reveal possible portfolio transfers.
- Hearst+ impressions, starts, completions, saves, returns, and outbound clicks for the same inventory.

### Evaluate incrementality, not just attribution

Use randomized exposure or a defensible holdout where feasible. Compare eligible audiences who saw the source-forward discovery treatment with a control group who did not. Measure source-side outcomes, Hearst+ outcomes, and the combined portfolio result. Segment by originating brand, format, reader tenure, and destination. A UTM report tells which channel was credited for visits; it cannot establish the counterfactual of what a reader would otherwise have done.

| Level | Primary outcomes | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Originating brand** | Incremental engaged visits, ad revenue/yield, subscription and registration value, and agreed in-app revenue credit | No material decline beyond the brand’s pre-agreed tolerance in its protected site or revenue outcomes |
| **Hearst+** | Repeat useful visits, reader satisfaction, content discovery, and licensed-content value | Do not increase app engagement by silently suppressing source referrals or source identity |
| **Portfolio** | Net new audience, total monetization, and cross-brand discovery | A portfolio gain cannot offset an unapproved loss at an individual title |

Set thresholds, confidence requirements, measurement windows, and pause triggers with the participating brands before results are visible. If one brand fails its threshold, change or pause that brand’s content mode; do not hide the result in the 29-brand average. Include a review cadence and an exit/withdrawal process in the agreement.

### Roll out in stages

1. **Governance:** Name a portfolio owner and brand contacts across editorial, analytics, advertising, subscriptions, SEO, legal, and privacy. Agree participation rights and value-credit definitions.
2. **Baseline:** Validate source-side event coverage and revenue definitions for each pilot brand. Confirm referral parameters survive redirects and are visible to the publisher.
3. **Preview pilot:** Start with a small, representative set of brands and source-forward previews. Test whether the experience creates incremental engaged visits and new brand discovery.
4. **Licensed-format pilot:** Separately test full in-app articles, video, and galleries only when approved. Track source-site outcomes and the contracted in-app credit.
5. **Scale by evidence:** Expand, revise, or stop separately by brand and format. Re-evaluate when recommendation rules, ad models, search treatment, or audience behavior materially change.

## Business-model scorecard

The model is in good shape only if all of these statements are true:

- Hearst+ gives readers a distinct discovery benefit that individual brand sites do not already provide.
- A reader can identify the originating brand and reach the original in one clear action.
- Every format has documented rights, search handling, ad treatment, and publisher value rules.
- Source-site referrals and destination-side engagement can be reported separately from in-app consumption.
- Each brand can see its own incremental outcomes and has a way to pause participation.
- Hearst+ engagement is not counted as a site visit, and source credit is not represented as 100% causal attribution.
- The rollout is held to per-brand thresholds, so portfolio gains cannot conceal a title-level loss.

## Bottom line

The strongest defensible model is **discovery first, licensed convenience second, and publisher value measured and shared throughout**. Source-forward previews make referral traffic an intentional product behavior. Licensed in-app consumption can make the experience more convenient, but it needs explicit rights and an explicit economic credit. Canonical metadata supports source identity and search hygiene; it cannot by itself make the aggregator beneficial.
