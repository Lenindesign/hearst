# Hearst Prototype App Rules

These rules capture durable product and interface decisions for the Hearst reader prototypes. Apply them across desktop and mobile unless a later decision explicitly replaces them.

Before making UI changes, also read `PRODUCT.md` for product intent, `STYLE.md` for the shared visual and interaction rules, and `BRAND_STYLES.md` for native inheritance plus brand colors, fonts, and logos. Keep broadly reusable style decisions in `STYLE.md`; keep behavior and scoped product exceptions here.

## Theme and surfaces

- The default page canvas is `#F4F2EE`; content modules use white surfaces.
- AutoWeek uses dark neutral `#242424` for editorial and module-title text on light surfaces. Keep AutoWeek yellow `#FFC84E` for utility backgrounds, active navigation underlines, numbered badges, carousel indicators, and other brand accents; do not use the yellow as foreground title text on white.
- Hearst Fashion & Luxury, including `/hearst-flux/`, its category views, and its brand routes, loads in dark mode by default.
- Exception: the `Videos` tab inside the Hearst+ home destination (`/hearst-plus/`) loads its feed area and complete navigation header in dark mode by default because it is a video-index experience. The utility bar, masthead, category navigation, compact sticky navigation, controls, active states, and Hearst+ logo all use the scoped dark treatment. This exception must not change non-video tabs, other destinations, RSS/story pages, or readers. This tab intentionally shows every configured video source across supported sections and brands by default.
- Video feeds must be scoped contextually: the Hearst+ home `Videos` tab shows all configured video brands, destination pages show only video brands assigned to that destination, and brand pages keep their selected brand context while still showing the sibling video brands available inside that section. Do not let the all-destination video inventory leak into Lifestyle, Autos, Fashion & Luxury, Enthusiast & Wellness, or individual brand routes.
- The `Videos by brand` sidebar module filters only the active video queue in place. Entering the Hearst+ home `Videos` tab clears any article-river brand filter so the default view shows all configured video brands. Brand choices made after entering the Videos tab must not navigate to a brand route or collapse the queue into an empty state. The Hearst+ home `Videos` tab is an index, so it does not suppress items with article-river `Hide` preferences; those preferences still apply to the normal article river.
- `Your Daily Habit` module titles and topic labels use the active theme primary color through the shared sidebar-heading token. AutoWeek keeps its documented dark-neutral title exception, and the dark Videos surface keeps its scoped light-blue treatment.
- Every story row in `Your Daily Habit` and `Trending Across Brands` is an interactive story link. The complete row opens the same in-app reader used by carousel and river cards.
- Editorial story links follow the shared hover, focus, and focus-ring treatment owned by `STYLE.md`.
- On the dark Videos surface, all blue section titles and video-brand eyebrows use the light-blue `--hp-sidebar-heading` token; this treatment stays scoped to the Videos view and must not leak into RSS or reader pages.
- Video feed fallbacks may only count and render stories with playable video media. Do not use article-only local fallback stories as video inventory because that creates misleading source counts and empty video queues.
- Video playback must choose a direct H.264/AVC MP4 when one is available. Treat a URL as direct MP4 only when its final media path ends in `.mp4`; an adaptive URL ending in `.m3u8` remains HLS even when an earlier path segment contains `.mp4`. Play HLS natively on capable Apple devices and use the shared `hls.js` fallback elsewhere. Preserve orientation metadata from a dimension-bearing alternate transcoding when the compatible HLS selection omits width and height, so portrait-video modules retain their complete inventory. Every video surface uses the shared adaptive player and exposes a retryable error state instead of leaving a broken poster or inert control.
- Video caption limitations are disclosed as prototype fine print in the video surface footer. Do not block prototype playback solely because source caption metadata is unavailable, but keep captions/transcripts listed as a production content requirement.
- The Hearst+ home `Videos` tab sits flush with the footer when it reaches the bottom of the page. Do not introduce a light spacer between the black video exception surface and the footer.
- Every Hearst destination carousel blends current Personalize articles and playable API videos when they match the active destination, topic, brand, or reader signals. Before applying its format and brand-diversity safeguards, the carousel must rescore every eligible slide with the same personalization model as the river: followed topics and brands, saved and hidden stories, More Like This behavior, time of day, return visits, popularity, freshness, and diversity. Keep the five-slide format with three editorial anchors, one current article, and one playable video when both live formats are available; fill missing formats with the best contextual story. Brand pages remain restricted to their selected brand for live articles, and destination video inventory must remain section-scoped. Deduplicate live stories against the editorial pool. Outside the carousel, alternate live article and video formats when possible and cap live/API insertions below 30% of the blended river.
- Refresh the generated Lifestyle, Autos, Fashion & Luxury, and Enthusiast & Wellness RSS inventories once each day before the morning edition. Validate minimum inventory, unique source URLs, complete story metadata, no publication timestamp more than fifteen minutes in the future, and at least one story published within the previous 72 hours in every destination group before publishing. Accept publication dates only from source-article metadata, source structured data, or RSS `pubDate`; never substitute the import time or sitemap modification time for publication. Import author names from RSS creator fields when available, then enrich remaining source-backed stories from article metadata or JSON-LD before validating the catalog. Skip undated stories, and do not let a failed or stale import replace the last valid production catalog. Rank freshness from each story's real `publishedAt` timestamp; keep the editorial default as a modest baseline so current stories matching the reader's followed topics, brands, saves, and More Like This signals can lead the personalized edition.
- The stakeholder demo console uses white content panels and includes plain-language personalization and technology sections. Keep its product explanation synchronized with the implemented eligibility, scoring, return-visit, behavior, slideshow, diversity, and exclusion logic. Keep its technology summary grounded in the installed production stack and clearly distinguish working prototype capabilities from browser-local demo state or systems that are not yet integrated, so the console remains reliable for executive review.
- On refresh, do not render the default static river while the saved reader account and personalization profile are hydrating. Keep the masthead and navigation stable, show a neutral layout-preserving feed skeleton, and mount the river only after hydration so readers see one personalized content state instead of a static-to-dynamic story swap.
- Destination and brand rivers preserve access to the complete eligible RSS story catalog through demand-driven delivery. Keep the initial server payload capped for fast startup, then request one cached editorial page only when the reader approaches the shared river sentinel and the loaded buffer is nearly exhausted. Never download the remaining catalog merely because the browser is idle. Videos use the same demand-driven behavior only inside the Videos experience or when a visible video module explicitly needs another page.
- Every destination and publication river begins with a compact rendered batch, then appends four ranked cards at a time as the reader approaches the shared sentinel. Request at most one server page for each new sentinel demand, deduplicate it into the existing queue, reject non-advancing cursors, abort stale requests on route or filter changes and when the page is hidden or offline, and expose a polite loading, retry, or end-of-river status. The empty Saved experience makes no catalog pagination requests.
- On every publication route, the shared `Global Story Inventory` sidebar shows the complete section-wide inventory count for each sibling brand rather than the small contextual preview currently loaded into the active river. Counts use one cached, deduplicated inventory across the full editorial catalog, current Personalize recommendations, and playable video inventory. Selecting a sibling brand opens its canonical publication route; destination-wide feeds retain their contextual `Filter Brands` behavior and the Videos tab retains `Videos by brand`.
- Onboarding brand choices use counts from the complete generated eligible catalog rather than the capped page payload. Computing these counts must not trigger background story or video feed requests. Disable a true zero-inventory brand and explain that it is unavailable in the demo. Brand-page pagination controls keep at least a 24px interactive target.
- Search matches normalized Unicode word tokens rather than arbitrary substrings. Rank exact title and title-token matches before brand, topic, and tag matches; allow prefix matching only for sufficiently long query tokens so short terms such as `Audi` do not match `Audiobooks` or `Saudi`.
- Every modal or full-screen reader layer portals to a direct body-level overlay, makes all other body content inert and `aria-hidden`, locks background scroll without losing its prior state, and restores focus on close. Nested layers such as Ambient Reader and the fullscreen gallery isolate the reader beneath them so only the topmost layer is exposed. Backdrops are non-semantic; each layer presents one clearly named close control.
- Only the Delish publication route uses palette colors 1 through 5 for its five featured-story indicators. The active Delish indicator retains the wider state while keeping its assigned palette color. Every other publication and destination river keeps the original primary/muted indicator treatment; do not extend this exception to their action controls or surrounding theme.
- Video cards do not show a generic `Watch` chip in their metadata row. Every stacked video card places the source brand icon immediately before the brand and topic metadata; featured and dedicated video-index cards retain their existing duration and play controls.
- The Hearst+ home `Videos` tab promotes playable Delish videos whose selected transcoding reports `height > width` into a dedicated Delish Shorts carousel between the featured video and the recommended queue. The same live portrait inventory also appears directly below the lead story on `/lifestyle/delish/`, using the publication river's light surface tokens. In the general Hearst+ `For You` river, treat Delish Shorts as a ranked feed module: place it immediately after the highest-ranked eligible Delish editorial story so its location comes from the personalized river rather than a fixed card number, and inherit the river's light semantic surfaces. If that river contains no eligible Delish editorial story, do not show the Shorts module there. Lock the computed position for the active river context so background catalog loading cannot make the module jump or disappear while the reader scrolls; recompute only when the destination, filter, brand scope, or demo moment changes. Remove promoted portrait items from the standard river or video queue so they do not appear twice. The Videos rail remains visible for the all-brand and Delish filters and hides when another single-brand filter is active. Only cards in these carousels open the full-screen Delish Shorts viewer; standard video cards keep the existing story-reader behavior. The immersive viewer stays on the current route, supports upward swipe for the next short and downward swipe for the previous short across pointer, touch, and trackpad input, and visually carries that direction through the change: next sends the current video upward while its replacement enters from below, and previous reverses the motion. Retain the equivalent up/down keyboard and button controls, reduced-motion fallback, and focus restoration when closed. Under each short headline, include a clear action that closes the Shorts viewer and opens the same story in the in-app reader.
- Gallery stories retain their gallery behavior and imagery but do not show a generic `Gallery` type chip on river cards. Explicit photo/gallery stories use the standard river card treatment instead of the video-index treatment, even when the feed includes playable media metadata. The demo river should surface at least one visible standard gallery-card sample when the current story set contains one, so the model can be reviewed without waiting for infinite scroll.
- When an explicit photo/gallery story resolves at least five distinct source images, upgrade that river item to the rich photo-mosaic card while preserving its ranked position and the shared reader action model. Resolve only visible gallery candidates so ordinary stories and category navigation are not blocked; if the gallery payload is unavailable or has fewer than five images, keep the standard river card.
- Standard article stories do not show a generic `Article` type chip on river cards; retain the editorial signal, source brand, topic, and byline metadata.
- Every editorial story surface uses the shared byline resolver, including primary river cards, carousels, video cards, search results, daily-habit modules, trending lists, related-story modules, sidebars, and readers. Show the source writer name when RSS, source metadata, or JSON-LD provides one; use the publication-editors label only when no writer is available. The daily feed refresh must run author enrichment and byline-coverage validation after every inventory import so a refresh cannot silently erase known writer names.
- Every river-card summary displays a maximum of three lines with an ellipsis, including standard, specs, gallery, shopping, recipe, brand-spotlight, related-story, and live/API cards. Variants that already use a shorter limit may keep it. Preserve the full source summary in the story reader.
- Exclude the MotorTrend video `Qualcomm's Dynamic Electric Vehicle Charging: An Innovation Story` from every app surface, including destination carousels, rivers, video indexes, sidebars, and readers.
- Exclude the Men's Health story `I Didn’t Let Chronic Pain Stop Me From Losing 115 Pounds. Here’s How I Did It.` from every app surface, including destination carousels, rivers, rankings, sidebars, and readers.
- Exclude the Good Housekeeping video `Fresko_sealing_burgers_480.mov` from every app surface, including destination carousels, rivers, video indexes, sidebars, and readers.
- The standalone `/hearst-plus/live-feed/` and `/hearst-plus/motortrend-videos/` routes remain source-focused QA views. They must not become the primary navigation model for Hearst+.
- The reader modal normally follows the active article section rather than the page underneath it: Fashion & Luxury uses dark mode, while Lifestyle, Autos, and Enthusiast & Wellness use light mode. When an article is opened from its own publication route, the standard reader instead inherits that publication's registered logo, colors, headline font, body font, and semantic controls. Its lazy-loaded story queue, context rail, recommendations, story count, and filter targets must also stay inside that publication unless the reader explicitly switches to another destination from the reader masthead. This publication treatment remains scoped to brand-origin entries and must not replace destination theming for articles opened from general destination feeds.
- Switching sections inside an open reader immediately updates the complete modal theme, including its shell, rails, masthead, article surface, logo, and controls.
- The Fashion & Luxury logo is white when it appears in the dark reader masthead.
- Fashion & Luxury article surfaces use a dark editorial surface with light, readable text so the reading column remains part of the section's dark-mode experience.
- Article surfaces for Lifestyle, Autos, and Enthusiast & Wellness remain white with dark, readable text.
- An explicit reader theme toggle may override the section default for the current experience.

## Reader modal

- On mobile, the standard reader occupies the complete visual viewport and replaces app-level chrome with one reader header and one contextual category row. Desktop retains the centered, inset reader surface. While the reader is open, the app shell beneath it is inert and absent from the accessibility tree.
- The modal masthead shows the contextual Hearst section logo for the article being read.
- Every source-backed reader article places the source publication icon and brand name beside the editorial signal above the headline. Do not repeat the icon or brand name in the action bar; that row keeps topic, source-page writer byline, follow state, date, and story actions. Resolve the writer from source metadata or JSON-LD before using the imported story byline or publication-editors fallback, and label the row `By {writer}`. Render the date at the same type size as the byline as an unlabeled absolute value such as `July 20, 2026`; in the Ambient Reader hero it replaces the estimated read time beside the author. Prefer the publication timestamp resolved from source-page metadata or JSON-LD, with the imported RSS `pubDate` as the fallback. If none is valid, omit the date; never generate one from the current time or a sitemap modification time. Show an `Updated` timestamp only when the source provides a valid modified time at least fifteen minutes later than publication; never generate or infer an update time. Use Pacific time rather than relative-only labels.
- The category row below the masthead uses the categories for that contextual section.
- The other Hearst section controls in the masthead switch the article collection, logo, and category row inside the open modal. They do not navigate away from the current page or close the modal.
- Reader articles lazy-load as one continuous queue, while the masthead remains sticky.
- Article headlines should preserve the source copy but use responsive sizing that avoids awkward, overly heavy wrapping.
- The reader action bar sits directly below the article headline and before the introduction or article body across article, video, loading, and fallback states.
- Source-backed text articles expose a compact premium Ambient Reader action in that bar. Ambient Reader opens as a full-screen, distraction-free layer, inherits the active publication's registered logo, colors, headline font, and body font, defaults to the Airy reading density, and retains shared controls for changing density, light/dark theme, estimated reading time, progress, and close. Keep the control visible in a disabled loading or unavailable state while the complete body is being resolved so the feature is discoverable. Enable standard articles after at least four non-image content blocks; also enable image-led gallery stories when the source provides at least two non-image content blocks and three gallery images. Hide it for videos and stories without a source URL. Closing Ambient Reader returns to the unchanged position in the standard reader.
- Ambient Reader hero copy is vertically centered inside its color panel so short and medium headlines feel balanced against the image panel; do not bottom-anchor this text block.
- Ambient Reader hero copy must stay inside its own grid panel at every breakpoint. The text column needs `min-width: 0` behavior and safe headline wrapping so large publication headline fonts never render underneath or get clipped by the image panel.
- Ambient Reader uses one shared interaction and article-body architecture with four destination-specific opening compositions. Fashion & Luxury keeps the saturated split-cover spread; Autos uses an image-first cinematic opener with a structured dark information deck; Enthusiast & Wellness reverses the split and uses active color with condensed, kinetic headline treatment; Lifestyle uses a spacious, bordered magazine spread with an inset image. Each composition still inherits the source publication logo, colors, headline font, and body font. Destination art direction must not fork queue navigation, accessibility, density, theme, progress, image, or related-story behavior.
- Ambient Reader may include subtle previous and next article controls in its header. These controls switch through a destination-scoped, feed-ordered queue of complete Ambient Reader articles, skipping videos and stories without complete source text while preserving the logical order of the active section such as Autos. Preload a bounded forward/back window instead of loading the full section at once, preserve the full-screen Ambient Reader layer, reset reading progress to the top of the selected article, and update the safe `/read/{storyId}/?from=...` URL with `history.replaceState`. The same navigation must work from the keyboard and mobile gestures: Right Arrow or a left swipe advances to the next complete Ambient Reader article, while Left Arrow or a right swipe returns to the previous one when available. Swipe detection must favor vertical scrolling unless the gesture is clearly horizontal.
- Ambient Reader body images progressively preserve source aspect ratio after load. Wide images stay full width, while portrait or vertical images render centered in a narrower vertical frame with `object-contain` so the reader sees the available portrait crop instead of a forced landscape crop.
- Ambient Reader hero images should adapt by source ratio. Landscape hero images keep the standard split and cropped editorial cover treatment; portrait or vertical hero images use roughly one third of the desktop hero width and still fill the panel vertically with `object-cover`, allowing the copy panel to take the remaining width while avoiding letterboxed portrait heroes.
- Ambient Reader section headings that introduce an immediate image should keep stronger separation from the prior content but sit close to the image they label. Tighten only the heading-to-image pair; do not compress the full article rhythm globally.
- Ambient Reader image zoom should happen in the fullscreen image viewer, not on inline article images. Keep it native-feeling and low chrome: double-tap or pinch to zoom, drag to pan while zoomed, double-tap again to reset, and provide only a subtle hint.
- MotorTrend Ambient Reader hero panels use the MotorTrend red brand background with white headline, summary, eyebrow, byline/read-time metadata, and divider line. Do not reintroduce the dark-gray MotorTrend hero exception.
- In the Ambient Reader header, the MotorTrend logo renders in MotorTrend red on the light header and white only in dark mode.
- In the standard story reader header, the MotorTrend logo also renders in MotorTrend red unless a dark destination header explicitly requires a white logo treatment.
- The standard reader masthead nav reflects the reader scope. Destination-origin readers render the same ordered destinations as the global section switcher: Lifestyle, Autos, Fashion & Luxury, Enthusiast & Wellness. Brand-origin readers render the sibling brands inside the current section instead, using that section's configured source order; the active brand remains visible and active with `aria-current="page"`. On desktop and tablet, keep this list inside the available masthead width as a horizontal carousel with previous and next controls, a hidden native scrollbar, and automatic positioning of the active item. Clicking the active nav item is a no-op; clicking another item switches the in-modal reader queue without closing the modal. Brand-level switching must be backed by the complete section story catalog, not only the currently filtered page feed, so sibling brand options do not appear disabled merely because the page entered through one brand.
- The Ambient Reader footer may promote related ambient reads below the end-of-article marker. Rank same brand, topic, destination, and shared-tag matches before proximity; only include stories that already resolve to complete Ambient Reader bodies, and keep clicks inside the same full-screen reader layer.
- Ambient Reader commerce modules may appear across any destination or publication only when a verified collection matches the actual story and resolved article body. Every displayed product must have a named product, a real product image, a destination URL, and editorially relevant rationale; do not infer generic products from a broad category, reuse article imagery as a product image, or render an empty commerce module. Prototype links must plainly disclose when affiliate tracking is not enabled.
- While the standard story reader is open, pressing the unmodified `P` key opens Ambient Reader for the complete article currently nearest the reading position. Ignore the shortcut while focus is in an input, textarea, select, contenteditable surface, or textbox, and while another fullscreen reader or gallery layer is open. Expose the shortcut through the premium action's tooltip and `aria-keyshortcuts` metadata.
- In the standard reader action row, an unfollowed brand plus badge is an outlined icon treatment in the same muted color family as the other row icons. Reserve filled brand-color treatment for the followed/active state, and do not show a full-row stroke/ring around the brand metadata control in its active or focused state.
- The executive POC reader is the complete article destination. Resolve every source-backed text story into its full inline article body and media as it enters the lazy-loaded queue; do not render a preview-body substitute or a `Read the original article` exit link while content is loading or after it resolves.
- Gallery previews, the standard reader, reader preloads, and Ambient Reader share one bounded client article cache keyed by canonical source URL. Concurrent consumers reuse the same in-flight request and resolved article; failures are not cached so a later consumer can retry. Component unmounts may ignore a result but must not cancel a request still needed by another consumer.
- Source-backed gallery and listicle stories that provide slide payloads resolve those slides into the same inline reader body as standard articles. Preserve the intro text, slide images, captions, and credits instead of showing a complete-article failure only because the source body is slide-based.
- Autos Network stories that do not expose the standard Hearst `__NEXT_DATA__` body payload must fall back to their marked readable article container and extract paragraphs, section headings, and article images from that bounded container. Keep controls, video timestamps, and gallery-count chrome out of the reader body.
- Story reader URLs use `/read/{storyId}/`. When a story is opened from an in-app page, links must append a safe same-origin `from` path and closing the reader returns to that captured entry page. Direct/shared `/read/{storyId}/` URLs without a safe `from` fall back to the story’s source brand route. Never allow external or nested `/read/` return targets.
- As readers scroll through the continuous modal queue, the browser URL updates to the story currently nearest the reading position with `history.replaceState`. Do not remount the reader, reorder the queue, or add a new browser-history entry for every passively reached story.

## Featured carousel

- Only the active featured slide is operable and exposed to assistive technology. Inactive slides remain rendered for animation but are `inert`, `aria-hidden`, and removed from sequential focus.
- Run `npm run test:a11y:carousel` after changing featured-carousel slides, controls, motion, or responsive behavior. The browser regression must pass every slide transition at 1280px and 320px, confirm exactly one operable slide, confirm named selectors identify the current slide, and reject page-level horizontal overflow.
- On desktop and tablet destination pages, the Today&rsquo;s Edit rail is a distinct content module below the category navigation. Keep it inside the shared page container, aligned to the same outer width as the sidebar-and-river grid, with equal vertical spacing above and below. Its individual story modules use the same `16px` internal padding as the sidebar modules, and their content determines the rail height without an additional minimum height. Do not attach it visually to the main navigation or pull it into a full-bleed viewport strip.
- Featured carousel headlines display a maximum of three lines at every breakpoint. Truncate longer source headlines with an ellipsis in the carousel only; preserve the full source headline in story cards and the reader.
- House Beautiful featured-carousel headlines use a `1.12` line height plus a small bottom allowance so SangBleu Sunrise descenders remain visible inside the three-line clamp and its hidden-overflow boundary.
- Featured carousel summaries display a maximum of two lines at every breakpoint. Truncate longer summaries with an ellipsis in the carousel only; preserve the full summary in story cards and the reader.
- Headlines in the `Today's edit` strip display a maximum of three lines at every breakpoint, with an ellipsis when truncated. Preserve the full source headline in the destination story and reader.
- The featured carousel supports touch swiping on mobile with visible drag feedback.
- Vertical page scrolling must continue to work when a gesture begins inside the carousel.
- A tap opens the story; a completed horizontal swipe changes slides and must not accidentally open a story.
- Carousel content must fit the mobile viewport without horizontal page overflow.
- Keep the Today&rsquo;s Edit rail compact at tablet and mid-desktop carousel widths. Before the four-column desktop grid takes over, cards should be narrow enough to reveal meaningful context from the next module rather than creating one oversized selected card.

## Navigation and branding

- Prototype account and identity UI must describe profile storage accurately. Local email profiles store feed, preference, and library state in the current browser. When `NEXT_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and server-only `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` are configured together, the Personalize dialog may use real Google One Tap; its credential must be verified server-side against the configured audience before a Google profile is created or resumed. Google profiles may sync prototype preferences, saved stories, collections, comments, and reading signals through the server profile store. Do not persist the Google credential.
- The `Personalize` entry point begins optional profile onboarding: readers can create a local email profile or use Google to resume a synced prototype profile, then choose one or two interests and optional trusted brands before applying the feed. Browsing must remain available without a profile, and the account dialog must plainly distinguish browser-local email profiles from Google-synced prototype profiles. Keep the phone interest picker horizontally scrollable so the preview remains visible in the first modal viewport, preserve the two-interest limit, and return focus to the opener after every close path.
- Executive or shareable prototype routes must show a persistent, non-blocking demo disclosure that states the surface is prototype-only, uses public story metadata, and relies on browser-local demo state. Do not present the app as a production account, access-controlled, or publishing surface until those systems exist.
- Destination and publication reader routes are content-first by default. Hide the floating personalization controls, source inventories, ranking-debug panels, simulation details, and Behavior Model section from ordinary readers. Stakeholder tools remain available only when the URL includes `?demo=1` or the explicit `NEXT_PUBLIC_HEARST_STAKEHOLDER_TOOLS=true` environment flag is set. Preserve demo mode through category, publication, and reader-return navigation without changing ranking or editorial content.
- On mobile destination pages, the left masthead control is a hamburger menu instead of the standalone theme toggle. The menu contains the complete Continue Reading queue, destination brand filter, and light/dark theme action. When genuine unfinished history exists, show only the most recent story in a compact one-line Continue Reading strip directly below the mobile category navigation; omit the strip when history is empty. Pin `Saved` at the right edge of the mobile category row so it never depends on horizontal discovery, while the remaining categories continue to scroll. Do not duplicate the full Today&rsquo;s Edit strip or brand-filter card in the mobile page flow; retain the existing strip, sidebar filter, and standalone masthead theme toggle on desktop.
- Continue Reading surfaces use browser-local history and show only stories the reader actually opened and has not finished. Never substitute catalog defaults. When no unfinished story exists, keep the mobile discovery menu available for brand filters and show a truthful empty-state message; omit the desktop Continue Reading module.
- When Saved has no stories, explain its value with `Save stories to build your reading list.`, provide a direct `Browse For You` action, and show exactly three ranked, unsaved, non-hidden suggestions with separate open and save actions. Saving a suggestion should immediately replace the empty state with the real Saved feed. If saved stories exist but an active brand filter excludes them, explain the mismatch and offer both Browse For You and Clear brand filter. Do not render an empty Trending Across Brands card beside an empty Saved state.
- On unfiltered destination `For You` views, the existing five-story featured carousel is the browser-local `Today’s Picks` edition. Keep its ranked, mixed-format selection stable for the local calendar day. Do not add a separate edition header or explanatory row; place `Today’s Picks` inside the existing slide-position pill beside `1 of 5`, using the same compact typography and treatment. Do not add progress bars, points, punitive streaks, or false urgency.
- On unfiltered destination `For You` views, allocate ranked inventory deterministically across Today&rsquo;s Picks, Today&rsquo;s Edit, Daily Habit, Trending Across Brands, and then the river. A story must not occupy more than one of those modules. Genuine Continue Reading may overlap Today&rsquo;s Picks only when no other unfinished story is available. Prefer different brands in Trending Across Brands, and preserve at least four stories for the river in a small scoped catalog by omitting optional modules before emptying the feed.
- Habit measurement uses the typed, vendor-neutral `hearst-product-analytics` browser event contract. It may include editorial content identifiers, destination, surface, position, format, brand, topic, edition ID, daypart, bounded return timing, and aggregate counts. Never include article text, search terms, comment bodies, email addresses, account IDs, source URLs, or other direct personal identifiers. The prototype does not send these events to an external service; any production collector requires a separate consent, retention, privacy, and data-governance decision. Count a useful session only after an explicit value action currently implemented as Save or More Like This; do not treat a page view or story click as success.
- Recommendation explanations appear only on `For You` river cards, one concise reason per card. Reasons must map to a real ranking input and use this priority: new since the last visit, new in today&rsquo;s edition, exact followed topic, exact followed brand, current daypart, editor selection, then broad Hearst popularity. Never invent reading time, user behavior, urgency, or a personalization claim that the current profile and visit context cannot support. Keep the explanation as quiet text metadata rather than a badge, tooltip, or additional card treatment.
- Reader ranking context derives from the actual local time and a browser-local visit record scoped to each destination or publication. Treat reloads within thirty minutes as the same visit. On a genuine return, restore elapsed time and the previous lead so the river can favor newly relevant stories and the featured carousel opens on a different pick without changing the day&rsquo;s five-story set. A changed local edition date activates next-day novelty against the current catalog; generated next-day story data remains stakeholder-simulation-only. Stakeholder time and return controls are explicit temporary overrides and must never overwrite real visit history.
- The destination masthead search opens an accessible, responsive autocomplete dialog rather than navigating to a separate search page. Match destination-scoped stories by title, brand, topic, and tag; respect the active brand filter; rank strong title matches first; and open the selected result in the existing in-app reader.
