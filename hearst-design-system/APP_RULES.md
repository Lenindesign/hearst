# Hearst Prototype App Rules

This file owns durable product behavior for the Hearst reader prototypes: feeds, personalization, readers, navigation, state, content eligibility, loading, measurement, and scoped workflow exceptions. Apply these rules across desktop and mobile unless a later canonical decision explicitly replaces them.

Read `PRODUCT.md` for intent. Open the relevant section of `STYLE.md` only when presentation changes, and the relevant section of `BRAND_STYLES.md` only when brand identity changes. Broadly reusable visual rules do not belong here.

## Story routes

Opening and closing a story must feel like lifting a page off the feed and putting it back, never like loading a new site.

- **Open in place.** From the river, carousel, rails, search or any in-app surface, the reader opens as a layer over the page already on screen (`openStory` in `home-page.tsx`). Only the address changes: a shallow history entry (`history.pushState` with `hearstReaderOverlay`) to `/read/<story>/?from=<page>`, so the link can be shared and Back works. Never `router.push` to `/read/` from an in-app surface: a full navigation unmounts the river, refetches it, and the reader renders twice.
- **Close returns to exactly where the reader left.** Close, Escape, the backdrop and Back step back to the page's own history entry. The page under the reader never unmounts, refetches or re-sorts, so scroll position, the surrounding stories and loaded pages are unchanged, and focus returns to the opener without scrolling. Re-ranking that follows a read (for example demoting read stories) applies on the next visit, not on return.
- **Back and Forward move the layer.** Back from `/read/` closes the reader; Forward reopens the same story, both without a page load.
- **Direct visits keep the full route.** Opening a `/read/` link directly or refreshing it renders the server reader page, and closing goes to its `from` page with the scroll snapshot as a fallback.
- **Tabs and sections** change the address with `history.pushState` too (no reload), and switching the story inside the reader replaces the entry instead of stacking new ones.
- Regression: open a story about 2,600px down the Hearst+ river and close it by button, Escape and Back. `scrollY` and the card's position must be unchanged to the pixel, and focus must be on the card's opener.

## Reader modal

Destination logos open their canonical destination front, while publication logos shown for brand-origin readers open the canonical publication front. Keep the logo's natural aspect ratio, normalized visual height, visible keyboard focus, and an explicit accessible link name.
- In the standard reader, tint the Hearst destination lockup with the active vertical's brand color across Autos, Lifestyle, Fashion & Luxury, and Enthusiast & Wellness. In dark mode, lighten that same hue enough to remain legible on the dark masthead; publication logos retain their registered treatment.
- The reader follows the shared layout contract: the layout gap (`--layout-gap`, 16px) between the context rail, article well and right rail, rails at `--layout-rail` (300px), and the page container padding 16 · 24 · 48 (base · md · xl). The ad rail joins at lg and the context rail at xl. Preserve the reader's existing vertical spacing and responsive stacking.
- Use the bookmark icon consistently for save and saved-item affordances across the app, including story controls, the reader action bar, Shorts and image viewers, and Saved Items module headings: outline when unsaved, filled when saved. Reserve the heart for reactions and favorites, such as community likes. Library navigation uses the books icon so it never reads as a save control.
- Every source-backed reader article places the source publication icon and brand name beside the editorial signal above the headline. Do not repeat the icon or brand name in the action bar; that row keeps topic, source-page writer byline, follow state, date, and story actions. Resolve the writer from source metadata or JSON-LD before using the imported story byline or publication-editors fallback, and label the row `By {writer}`. If none is valid, omit the date; never generate one from the current time or a sitemap modification time. Show an `Updated` timestamp only when the source provides a valid modified time at least fifteen minutes later than publication; never generate or infer an update time. Use Pacific time rather than relative-only labels.
- The category row below the masthead uses the categories for that contextual section.
- The other Hearst section controls in the masthead switch the article collection, logo, and category row inside the open modal. They do not navigate away from the current page or close the modal.
